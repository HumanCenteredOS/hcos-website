# HCOS™ Decision Checklist

## Purpose

The HCOS™ Decision Checklist is a concise reference designed to support thoughtful, human-centered decision-making.

Rather than replacing professional judgment, the checklist helps individuals and organizations pause, ask better questions, and ensure that important decisions have been considered from multiple perspectives before they are finalized.

It is intended to complement the:

- HCOS™ Decision Approach
- HCOS™ Decision Guide
- HCOS™ Decision Worksheet

The checklist may be used during:

- Strategic planning
- Policy development
- AI governance
- Clinical governance
- Patient safety reviews
- Operational planning
- Quality improvement initiatives
- Organizational change
- Project management

---

# The HCOS™ Decision Checklist

The HCOS™ Decision Checklist is organized around the three questions that define the HCOS™ Decision Approach.

Rather than viewing decision-making as a single event, HCOS encourages organizations to move through three complementary stages of thinking.

```text
                    HCOS™ Decision Checklist

             Understand Before You Judge

         ✓ PURPOSE

         ✓ ANALYSIS

                     ↓

        Assign Responsibility Fairly

         ✓ ACCOUNTABILITY

                     ↓

         Strengthen the System

         ✓ STEWARDSHIP

         ✓ TRANSPARENCY

         ✓ CONTINUOUS LEARNING
```

This structure helps ensure that understanding comes before judgment, accountability is assigned fairly, and improvement is pursued intentionally.

---

# 1. Understand

## Purpose

☐ Is the purpose of the decision clearly defined?

☐ Does the decision support the organization's mission and values?

☐ Have we identified who may be affected?

☐ Have we considered both short-term and long-term impacts?

---

## Analysis

☐ Do we understand what happened?

☐ Have we distinguished evidence from assumptions?

☐ Have we identified remaining uncertainty?

☐ Have we considered contributing system factors?

☐ Have we gathered appropriate perspectives?

☐ Have we identified any unintended consequences?

---

# 2. Assign Responsibility Fairly

## Accountability

☐ Is decision-making authority clearly identified?

☐ Do responsibilities appropriately match authority?

☐ Were adequate resources available?

☐ Were expectations reasonable?

☐ Has accountability been clearly communicated?

☐ Have implementation responsibilities been assigned?

---

# 3. Strengthen the System

## Stewardship

☐ Have we identified opportunities for improvement?

☐ Have we considered what should remain unchanged?

☐ Have we preserved valuable knowledge?

☐ Have we documented the rationale for the decision?

☐ Have we identified how success will be monitored?

---

## Transparency

☐ Could someone unfamiliar with this decision understand why it was made?

☐ Is supporting evidence documented?

☐ Are assumptions clearly identified?

☐ Is the reasoning transparent?

☐ Can this decision be reviewed in the future?

---

## Continuous Learning

☐ What have we learned?

☐ What should improve?

☐ What should be preserved?

☐ What should future leaders inherit?

☐ When will this decision be reviewed?

---

# Final Reflection

Before finalizing the decision, pause and ask:

- Have we sought understanding before judgment?
- Does accountability appropriately match authority?
- Does this decision respect human dignity?
- Have we considered the perspectives of those affected?
- Does this decision strengthen trust?
- Have we documented our reasoning?
- Will future leaders understand why this decision was made?
- Does this decision strengthen both today's work and tomorrow's system?

## Guiding Principle

> **Healthy systems understand before they judge, assign accountability fairly, and steward improvement intentionally.**

The HCOS™ Decision Checklist is intended to support thoughtful governance by encouraging organizations to ask better questions before making important decisions. It promotes reflection, transparency, accountability, and continuous learning while remaining adaptable across healthcare, artificial intelligence, business, education, nonprofit organizations, and other human systems.
