# HCOS™ Decision Examples

## Purpose

The HCOS™ Decision Examples demonstrate how the HCOS Decision Approach can be applied across a variety of human systems.

The examples are intentionally simplified to illustrate the decision-making process rather than prescribe specific organizational actions.

Although every situation is unique, the underlying questions remain remarkably consistent:

- What happened?
- Who is responsible for what happens next?
- How should the system improve?

---

# Example 1 — Healthcare

## Situation

A hospital experiences an increase in delayed medication administration after implementing a new electronic health record workflow.

### Analysis

Questions explored:

- What changed?
- Was adequate training provided?
- Did workflow design contribute?
- Were staffing levels appropriate?
- Did the technology introduce new complexity?

Rather than assuming individual error, the organization evaluates the broader system.

---

### Accountability

Questions explored:

- Who approved the workflow?
- Who was responsible for implementation?
- Did clinicians receive adequate education?
- Were expectations reasonable?

Responsibility is aligned with authority rather than assigned solely to frontline staff.

---

### Stewardship

Potential improvements:

- Simplify the workflow.
- Improve training.
- Monitor medication timing.
- Gather clinician feedback.
- Preserve successful workflow elements.

# Example 2 — Artificial Intelligence

## Situation

An AI system recommends an incorrect clinical action that is identified by a healthcare professional before reaching the patient.

### Analysis

Questions explored:

- Why did the recommendation occur?
- Was context missing?
- Did the AI have sufficient information?
- Were confidence levels communicated?
- Were workflow assumptions valid?

---

### Accountability

Questions explored:

- Who approved deployment?
- Who maintains the model?
- What level of human oversight was expected?
- Were governance processes followed?

---

### Stewardship

Potential improvements:

- Improve validation.
- Refine governance.
- Update implementation guidance.
- Improve user education.
- Enhance monitoring.

# Example 3 — Leadership

## Situation

Employee engagement scores decline following organizational restructuring.

### Analysis

Questions explored:

- What concerns are employees expressing?
- How has communication changed?
- Were workloads redistributed?
- Were managers adequately supported?

---

### Accountability

Questions explored:

- Who approved the restructuring?
- Were expectations communicated?
- Were managers given appropriate authority?

---

### Stewardship

Potential improvements:

- Improve communication.
- Review workload distribution.
- Increase leadership visibility.
- Reassess organizational support.

# Example 4 — Technology

## Situation

A new software platform is delivered on schedule but adoption remains low.

### Analysis

Questions explored:

- Was the technology solving the right problem?
- Were users involved during design?
- Was implementation adequately supported?

---

### Accountability

Questions explored:

- Who defined project success?
- Were implementation responsibilities clear?
- Were adequate resources available?

---

### Stewardship

Potential improvements:

- Increase user participation.
- Simplify workflows.
- Improve onboarding.
- Monitor adoption.

# Example 5 — Education

## Situation

Students perform well on standardized assessments but report feeling increasingly disconnected from learning.

### Analysis

Questions explored:

- What measures define success?
- Are current metrics capturing meaningful learning?
- Have student perspectives been considered?

---

### Accountability

Questions explored:

- Who establishes educational priorities?
- How are teachers supported?
- Are expectations realistic?

---

### Stewardship

Potential improvements:

- Expand measures of learning.
- Increase student engagement.
- Preserve successful instructional practices.

# Example 6 — Business

## Situation

A company introduces aggressive productivity targets that improve short-term performance but increase employee turnover.

### Analysis

Questions explored:

- What incentives influenced behavior?
- Did metrics create unintended consequences?
- Were employee perspectives considered?

---

### Accountability

Questions explored:

- Who established the performance metrics?
- Were managers given appropriate flexibility?
- Did expectations align with available resources?

---

### Stewardship

Potential improvements:

- Reevaluate organizational metrics.
- Balance productivity with sustainability.
- Improve feedback mechanisms.
- Monitor workforce well-being.

# Common Pattern

Although these examples involve different industries, each follows the same HCOS Decision Approach.

```text
Understand Before You Judge

↓

Analysis

Why did this happen?

↓

Accountability

Who is responsible for what happens next?

↓

Stewardship

How should the system improve?
```

The framework remains consistent even when the circumstances change.

# Reflection

As you consider your own organization, ask:

- Which decisions would benefit from a more structured approach?
- Do discussions begin with understanding or with judgment?
- Does accountability appropriately match authority?
- Are opportunities for learning intentionally captured?
- How might stewardship strengthen future decisions?
